\begin{abstract}
This intermediate report exposes the party--district accounting cells used to
construct the coalition decomposition. It links the complete election-year
panel to the observed-cabinet and ideological-interval inversion registries,
while keeping calculation, validation, and presentation in a reproducible
pipeline. All empirical tables and all case-specific findings in this document
are generated from machine-readable intermediaries. There are no manually
entered numerical rows in this source.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and evidentiary status}

The report is an empirical diagnostic rather than part of the manuscript. Its
purpose is to make visible how national party and coalition differentials
accumulate across electoral units, and to identify patterns that may deserve
substantive discussion later. The generated tables can subsequently be input
individually into another \LaTeX{} document, but this report does not copy or
synchronize any asset into the manuscript.

The decomposition is strictly an \emph{accounting identity}. It organizes an
observed seat differential into internally consistent components; it does not
identify a causal effect of an electoral rule, a party, a district, or a
coalition. Rankings and sign patterns in the generated tables are therefore
descriptive leads for interpretation, not estimates of responsibility.

\section{Accounting definitions}

Let $i$ index parties and $d$ index the electoral units used by the validated
pipeline. Party $i$ receives $v_{id}$ valid federal-deputy votes and is
attributed $s_{id}$ federal-deputy seats in district $d$. District totals are
$V_d=\sum_i v_{id}$ and $S_d=\sum_i s_{id}$; the corresponding national totals
are $V=\sum_d V_d$ and $S=\sum_d S_d$. The party--district accounting cells are

\begin{align}
a_{id}
  &=s_{id}-S_d\frac{v_{id}}{V_d},\\
b_{id}
  &=S_d\frac{v_{id}}{V_d}-S\frac{v_{id}}{V}.
\end{align}

The first cell compares the seats attributed to a party with its exact
within-district quota. A positive $a_{id}$ means that the attributed seat count
is above that benchmark; a negative value means it is below. The second cell
compares the district quota contribution with the same votes' contribution to
a single national quota. Its equivalent factored form is

\begin{equation}
b_{id}
=S\left(\frac{S_d}{S}-\frac{V_d}{V}\right)
 \frac{v_{id}}{V_d}.
\end{equation}

This expression helps interpret the sign of $b_{id}$, but it does not isolate a
single institutional mechanism. In particular, $B$ must not be described as
``pure malapportionment.'' It combines the district seat weights present in
national aggregation, differences in turnout and valid-vote totals, and the
geographic distribution of party or coalition support. District magnitude can
also shape the allocation environment represented in $A$.

The party aggregates are

\begin{equation}
A_i=\sum_d a_{id},\qquad
B_i=\sum_d b_{id},\qquad
d_i=A_i+B_i=s_i-S\frac{v_i}{V}.
\end{equation}

For any reported coalition $C$, summing over its member parties gives

\begin{equation}
A_C=\sum_{i\in C}A_i,\qquad
B_C=\sum_{i\in C}B_i,\qquad
d_C=A_C+B_C=s_C-S\frac{v_C}{V}.
\end{equation}

The generated closure tables and case outputs provide the numerical checks for
these identities. The report uses the electoral units, party crosswalks, seat
attributions, and valid-vote denominators already established by the corrected
pipeline; it does not substitute a new denominator or reconstruct seats by a
different rule.

\subsection{Party attribution and coalition domains}

Party and party--district entries must be read as ex post accounting
attributions. In 2014 and 2018, seats were often allocated through joint
electoral lists, so the entries do not describe autonomous party-level
seat-allocation mechanisms. In 2022, electoral federations likewise require an
ex post qualification when results are displayed under individual party
labels. These qualifications apply even though the accounting equalities are
exact.

Observed cabinet cases and ideological cases answer different questions. A
cabinet case uses the corrected contemporaneous cabinet reconstruction. An
ideological case is a synthetic connected interval in the validated ideological
ordering restricted to Chamber-represented parties. The all-party ordering is
retained in separate robustness outputs. Both specifications measure coalition
vote shares against all valid federal-deputy votes, including zero-seat parties. Ideological cases can
overlap or nest, so a party--district cell may enter several case summaries;
those repetitions are not independent political observations. The ideological
registry in this report contains inversion cases only. Negative comparisons and all admissible coalitions for both ideological
universes are retained in the machine-readable pipeline outputs.
